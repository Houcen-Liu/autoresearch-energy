\title[Self-Hosted LLMs under an Agentic AutoML Workload]{
	Evaluating Self-Hosted LLM Architectures under an Agentic AutoML Workload: An Energy/Accuracy Pareto Study of \texttt{autoresearch}
}
\subtitle{Project Proposal — 6 ECTS Research Project (draft, results pending execution)}

\author{Houcen Liu}
\affiliation{%
 \institution{Vrije Universiteit Amsterdam}
 \city{Amsterdam}
 \country{The Netherlands}
} \email{houcenliu@gmail.com}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent \textit{Context}.
Self-hosting large language models promises privacy, cost control, and independence from frontier-API providers, but its viability hinges on whether locally-served models deliver acceptable accuracy at acceptable energy cost on realistic workloads. Agentic loops, in which an LLM repeatedly proposes, evaluates, and revises actions, are among the most demanding and least-measured of such workloads.

\noindent \textit{Goal}.
We evaluate whether a simple self-hostable model architecture, a sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) model compared against a dense model of the same family, can drive an agentic AutoML loop with good performance and energy efficiency, and we identify the intra-model and agent-loop configurations that improve on the baseline energy/accuracy profile. Karpathy's \texttt{autoresearch} is the case-study workload.

\noindent \textit{Method}.
A controlled experiment following the Green Lab methodology, staged: Stage~1 studies the architecture at a fixed intra-model configuration via a $2 \times 2 \times 2$ full-factorial design (proposer architecture $\times$ patience $\times$ loop budget) with 3 repetitions (24 sessions of agent-driven CIFAR-10 CNN training, $\approx$10 GPU-hours as executed), orchestrated by \texttt{experiment-runner} with \texttt{EnergiBridge} energy profiling; Stage~2 varies intra-model configurations on the better architecture for further optimization. Training and proposer inference run on physically separate GPUs for clean per-component energy attribution. A CPU-only proposer-serving phase assesses whether performance slowdowns remain acceptable for self-hosters deploying without a GPU.

\noindent \textit{Results}.
The sparse proposer dominates: it consumed 42\,\% less energy per session
(90.7 vs.\ 156.4\,kJ; ART-ANOVA $p = 7\times10^{-6}$, partial $\eta^2 = 0.73$)
\emph{and} reached higher test accuracy (0.8224 vs.\ 0.7939), retaining 17 improvements
against 12. The mechanism is multiplicative: $\sim$3B active parameters draw
36\,\% less instantaneous power and complete a proposal in half the time, for
2.4$\times$ less energy per retained improvement. Reliability separates the arms
more sharply than central tendency: the dense proposer ended 5 of 12 sessions
below its own starting point, against 1 of 12 for the MoE. Doubling the iteration
budget raised energy per retained improvement by 108\,\% (the only contrast
surviving Holm correction); patience had no detectable effect.

\noindent \textit{Conclusions}.
On a single 20\,GB card, sparsity buys energy efficiency on an agentic workload
without costing accuracy; the trade-off the architecture is usually assumed to
impose did not appear. Two methodological results generalize beyond the subjects:
sampling temperature changes how often a proposer repeats itself (23 duplicate
proposal pairs at $T=0$ against 2--9 above it) without changing what a session
achieves, and a mid-experiment diagnosis we made on that basis proved wrong when
tested; and a keep threshold must be derived from a measured noise
floor, since the value assumed a priori sat four times below it.
\end{abstract}
