\section{Conclusions}\label{sec:conclusions}

On a single 20\,GB GPU, a sparse Mixture-of-Experts proposer drove an agentic
AutoML loop using \textbf{42\,\% less energy per session than a dense model of
comparable serving footprint, while reaching higher accuracy} (0.8224 vs.\
0.7939 test accuracy, 17 retained improvements against 12). The energy advantage
is larger still on the component the architecture actually governs: 62\,\% less
proposer energy, and 2.4$\times$ less energy per retained improvement. The
trade-off that motivated the study, cheaper inference paid for with worse
steering, and therefore more wasted downstream training, did not appear.
Sparsity also proved the more \emph{reliable} choice: the dense proposer ended 5
of 12 sessions below its own starting point, against 1 of 12 for the MoE.

Of the two agent-loop factors, only loop budget mattered: doubling it raised
energy per retained improvement by 108\,\%, because the improvements that survive
are found early. Patience showed no effect at either level, which we attribute to
proposals being large and non-compositional rather than to the mechanism being
wrong in principle. Read as configuration guidance, the frontier says that at any
energy budget above 56.5\,kJ the most accurate configuration available is an MoE
configuration at the smaller loop budget.

Two methodological results generalise beyond these subjects. First, \textbf{a
keep threshold must be measured, not assumed}: the value we specified a~priori
sat four times below the workload's noise floor, at which point a retained
mutation is indistinguishable from re-running identical code. Second,
\textbf{sampling temperature governs how often a proposer repeats itself without
governing what it achieves}: duplicate proposal pairs fall from 23 at $T=0$ to
between 2 and 9 above it, while retention and accuracy stay flat across $[0,1]$.

We reached the second of these by first getting it wrong. A degenerate search at
temperature 0 was our explanation for three early sessions that retained nothing;
a later experiment showed temperature 0 retains mutations normally, and that
three all-dense sessions retaining nothing is an ordinary draw from an arm that
retains nothing 42\,\% of the time. The cost of that error was a restart, twelve
sessions to disentangle an intervention that had changed two things at once, and
a claim we had to withdraw. We record it because the alternative, reporting only
the corrected account, would misrepresent how the study was actually conducted.

We also note a dissociation with implications for how agentic systems are
benchmarked: contract compliance and proposal quality are independent axes. The
MoE violated the output contract more often than the dense model and produced
better recipes; in the pilot a 4B model was rejected on two thirds of its
iterations and still improved accuracy, while a 14B model complied perfectly and
improved nothing. Benchmarks scoring format compliance or tool-call validity
would order these pairs backwards.

\textbf{Limitations.} One workload at one training budget, one model family, one
quantization method, and $n = 12$ per arm: effect sizes are large and consistent,
but the corrected pairwise test for the architecture contrast does not reach
significance. The two arms differ in total capacity (30.5B vs.\ 14.8B), so
sparsity and capacity are not fully separable; the claim is that the sparse model
is more efficient per joule \emph{on one 20\,GB card}, not that sparsity is
superior at matched capacity. Results are conditional on this harness, which
demonstrably shapes them.

\textbf{Extensions}, in the order we would prioritise them. (i) Reasoning as a
factor: probing shows it inflates output 2.2--2.8$\times$ with throughput
unchanged, and, combining that with measured power, the sparse model can
reason for slightly \emph{less} energy than the dense model spends not reasoning
(6.17 vs.\ 6.73\,kJ per proposal), so whether reasoning improves proposal quality
is the natural next question. (ii) A long-horizon session: the agent recovers
$\approx$67\,\% of the headroom to a hand-tuned reference within 20 iterations,
and whether the remainder is reachable, or whether the loop begins overfitting the
validation split it selects on, is measurable by replaying retained revisions
against the test set post hoc. (iii) CPU-only proposer serving, where we expect
the architectures to diverge sharply, since $\sim$3B active parameters are far
better suited to CPU inference than 14.8B dense ones. (iv) Wider workload and
model-family axes, including \texttt{nanochat} as a second subject, to test
whether the frontier's shape is workload-invariant. The session-level
orchestration pattern, harness and replication package are designed to make each
a drop-in continuation.
