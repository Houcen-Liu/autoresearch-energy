\section{Results}\label{sec:results}

All 24 Phase-1 sessions completed and were valid: no session exceeded the 25\,\%
infrastructure-error threshold, and none was quarantined. The registered
inferential outcome is gross NVML GPU-board energy; no idle subtraction is
applied. Archived EnergiBridge traces also expose an AMD CPU-package counter, so
a timestamp-aligned GPU+CPU-package sensitivity total is reported. It excludes
DRAM and other host components and is not called full-system energy. Accuracy is
CIFAR-10 test-set accuracy measured once per session on the retained recipe.
The measured-component sensitivity reproduces the registered energy inference:
ART gives $F=42.42$, $p=7.15\times10^{-6}$ and partial $\eta^2=0.726$ for proposer.

\textbf{Is this simply a speed result?} Figure~\ref{fig:diagnostics}(a) separates
duration from power. Dividing each arm's mean energy by its mean duration gives
103.2\,W for dense and 88.7\,W for MoE, a 16.4\,\% difference; mean duration
differs by 48\,\%. The dense/MoE energy ratio therefore decomposes as
$1.48 \times 1.16 = 1.72$. The fitted session-level slopes (103.2 and 95.3\,W)
answer a different regression question and are not used in this ratio identity.
The larger phase-specific power difference is concentrated where expected: during
proposal phases the devices draw 112.8\,W (dense) against 72.5\,W (MoE), and
$E_{\mathit{prop,phase}}$ per proposal differs by $2.7\times$. Reporting only session
energy would understate the architectural effect; reporting only proposer power
would overstate it.

Figure~\ref{fig:diagnostics}(c) addresses run order: energy correlates with
position in the run table at $r = -0.05$, so no thermal or environmental drift
accumulated across the campaign that randomisation would have needed to absorb.

Proposer architecture and loop budget both carry large effects on GPU session
energy ($\eta^2_p = 0.73$ and $0.76$); patience does not, and no two-way energy
interaction is detected. A matching session-level factorial model for test
accuracy estimates MoE minus dense at $+2.85$\,pp (95\,\% CI

superiority is not; nonsignificance is not an equivalence test.

of GPU energy per keep for budgets 10 and 20: an increase of 146.2\,\% (stratified
bootstrap ratio CI $[1.29,4.46]$). Patience changes the pooled value from 92.9
to 115.5\,kJ ($+24.3\,\%$), but its interval includes both directions.

\subsection{Agent behaviour}

\textbf{Contract compliance and proposal quality are independent axes.} The
guard rejected 0.0\,\% of dense proposals and 9.6\,\% of MoE proposals (maximum
30\,\% in one session), yet the MoE produced the better recipes. A benchmark
scoring only output-format compliance would rank these two models in the opposite
order. The same dissociation appeared in the pilot, where a 4B model was rejected
on four of six iterations and still gained $+8.8$\,pp while a 14B model complied
perfectly and gained nothing.

\textbf{Failure to run is a dependent variable.} Sessions averaged 4.1 (dense) and
3.3 (MoE) crashed proposals, with single-session extremes of 19/20 and 15/20.
Because a crashed proposal consumes a full proposer inference and returns
nothing, crash rate feeds directly into energy efficiency. A representative
failure: the dense proposer replaced
\texttt{SGD} at \texttt{lr=0.01} with \texttt{AdamW} at the same rate, retaining a
learning rate roughly an order of magnitude too large for Adam, and reached
$0.0924$ accuracy.

\textbf{Thinking mode was verified, not assumed.} Summing reasoning tokens over
all 360 proposals in both arms yields zero, confirming that
\texttt{enable\_thinking:\,false} was honoured by the serving stack rather than
merely accepted.

\subsection{Stage 2a: does reasoning pay for itself?}\label{sec:results:reasoning}

Reasoning mode is a fixed variable in Stage~1 (disabled for both arms, verified
at zero tokens across all 360 Phase-1 proposals). Stage~2a promotes it to a
factor: \texttt{proposer} $\times$ \texttt{thinking}, 3 repetitions, greedy,
budget~10. All 12 sessions were valid. The manipulation is confirmed by
measurement rather than by configuration: every \texttt{off} session emitted
exactly 0 reasoning tokens and every \texttt{on} session between 23\,909 and
47\,904.

On log proposer-device energy, reasoning multiplies energy by $2.24\times$ for
dense (HC3 95\,\% CI $[1.80,2.78]$) and $2.67\times$ for MoE
($[2.17,3.28]$); the ratio-of-ratios is 1.19 ($[0.88,1.61]$, $p=0.214$).
For gross session GPU energy the multipliers are 1.59 ($[1.25,2.03]$) and 1.76
($[1.35,2.29]$), with interaction ratio 1.10 ($[0.77,1.58]$). Adding CPU-package
energy gives 1.54 ($[1.18,2.02]$) and 1.80 ($[1.42,2.29]$), with interaction
1.17 ($[0.82,1.67]$). Reasoning therefore raises energy in both arms; there is
no clear arm-specific multiplicative energy interaction.

\textbf{The exploratory accuracy interaction is consistent with substitution.} In a
session-level $2\times2$ model, enabling reasoning changes dense test accuracy by
$+9.18$\,pp (HC3 95\,\% CI $[5.74,12.62]$) and MoE accuracy by $-0.91$\,pp
($[-6.42,4.60]$). The architecture-by-reasoning difference-in-differences is
$-10.09$\,pp ($[-16.58,-3.59]$). With three sessions per cell this is an
exploratory interaction, not a general claim that reasoning and sparsity are
substitutes.

\textbf{The practitioner reading inverts the intuitive one.} The cheapest
configuration measured, MoE without reasoning, attains 0.8469 test accuracy for
52.1\,kJ, against dense with reasoning at 0.8490 for 172.6\,kJ: the MoE uses
\textbf{69.8\,\% less GPU energy} (dense uses $3.31\times$ as much) for an
observed difference of 0.21\,pp. The direct accuracy
contrast is imprecise (95\,\% CI $[-3.97,4.38]$\,pp, $p=0.912$); it does not
establish equivalence. The energy separation is nevertheless large.

We registered a quantitative prediction before running, derived from the
feasibility probe and the measured proposal-phase power draws: that the MoE could
reason for less energy than the dense model spends not reasoning (6.17 vs.\
6.73\,kJ per proposal). The direction held, but the observed values were 5.90
and 5.92\,kJ (4.4\,\% and 12.0\,\% below prediction), so the margin collapsed
from 0.56 to 0.02\,kJ. These point estimates are nearly equal; no equivalence margin or test
was pre-specified, so equality is not claimed.

Finally, observed mean coding failures rose
in both arms (dense 1.0~$\rightarrow$~4.3, MoE 3.3~$\rightarrow$~4.3). We had
expected a reasoning pass to catch errors such as retaining an SGD learning rate
when switching to AdamW. At $n=3$ per cell this pattern is descriptive, but it
provides no evidence of an error reduction.

cap was non-binding in these runs; this is a near-replication rather than a
literally identical configuration.

\subsection{Stage 2b: sampling temperature, and a correction}\label{sec:results:temperature}

Phase~1 was restarted once because its first three sessions kept no mutations at
all, which we attributed at the time to sampling temperature 0 making the search
degenerate. Stage~2b tests that attribution directly: four temperatures, three
repetitions, MoE arm, against the current prompt.

\centering

\midrule

\textbf{Temperature changes proposal duplication; outcome evidence is inconclusive.} Byte-identical
proposal pairs fall from 23 at $T=0$ to between 2 and 9 above it, and the fraction
of distinct proposals within a session rises from 0.77 to 0.97. Retained
mutations (1.33--2.33) and best observed accuracy (0.8235--0.8361) show no
corresponding descriptive pattern. One $T=0$ session produced only 4 distinct proposals out of
10 and still retained 2 mutations, reaching 0.8486. Duplicate proposals waste
iterations; this example shows that duplication does not necessarily prevent a
session from retaining an improvement.

\textbf{The original attribution is not supported as a sufficient mechanism.} At $T=0$ against the current prompt
the MoE retains 1.67 mutations per session, which is the normal rate for that
arm. Thus $T=0$ is not sufficient to force zero retention under the current
prompt. Because the restart changed both prompt content and temperature, these
data cannot exclude a temperature-by-prompt interaction or isolate either change.

Two other findings account for what we saw. First, the three sessions that
prompted the restart were, by the shuffled run order, all dense
($P = 0.11$ for the first three runs of a balanced 24-row table). Second, the
dense arm retains nothing in 5 of its 12 Phase-1 sessions even at $T=0.7$ with
the corrected prompt, a rate of 0.42. Three consecutive zero-retention dense
sessions is therefore an ordinary draw rather than evidence of a broken search.

\textbf{What the prompt did.} The restart also added a measured statement of the
training budget to the prompt. That change is not neutral, but its effect is
arm-specific: of the dense arm's \texttt{T\_max} literals in Stage 2a, 19 of 20
still specify more than 60 epochs for a budget that delivers roughly 43, whereas
the MoE emits none out of 82 in Stage 2b. The dense proposer continues to design
for a training run it is not given, after being told what it is given.

\subsection{Stage 2c: the same check on the dense arm}\label{sec:results:dense-temp}

Stage 2b varies temperature on the MoE, whereas the sessions that prompted the
restart were dense. Stage 2c repeats the check on that arm at the two levels that
matter, $T \in \{0, 0.7\}$, with three repetitions. It is a mechanism check
rather than a second sweep: with the dense arm retaining nothing in 42\,\% of
sessions, three repetitions cannot resolve an effect on retention, whereas the
duplication effect measured on the MoE (23 pairs against 2) is large enough to
detect.

\textbf{Duplication responds to temperature on both arms.} The dense arm shows
9 byte-identical proposal pairs at $T=0$ and none at $T=0.7$, with the distinct

on both subjects.

\textbf{No outcome effect can be estimated from this check.} Retention was 1.00 at $T=0$ against
0.33 at $T=0.7$, if anything the opposite of what the retracted claim predicts,
and best-observed accuracy differs by 3.3\,pp in favour of $T=0.7$ with both
levels containing sessions whose best evaluated candidate remained below their
starting validation baseline. At $n=3$ on an
arm this variable, none of these differences is interpretable. What the check
shows only that $T=0$ can produce retained improvements under the current prompt;
it does not establish a null outcome effect.

Two observations from these six sessions bear on the dense arm rather than on
temperature. In three of the six, even the best evaluated candidate remained
\emph{below} the starting validation baseline, and the
two worst ($-10.3$\,pp and $-6.9$\,pp) recorded no errors at all: every proposal
